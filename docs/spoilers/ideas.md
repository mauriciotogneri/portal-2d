# Idea Pool

Chamber ideas with no chamber yet, by element and part (`docs/level_design.md`, guideline 58). They give away key moves, so, like each chamber's notes in this folder, they are for designing, not for a player who will play the chamber cold. Prose sketches, not layouts: none has been built, solved or tested, but the cut drafts at the end, which were built, checked and played before they were cut. Each names its part (teach, test, twist, combine, as in guideline 41) and the guidelines to check when building it. When an idea becomes a chamber, move it into that chamber's notes; when a draft is cut, add its idea here, tagged by its elements and part.

What exists already, so the ideas avoid repeating it: the campaign's old chambers, now Playground 2 to 10, taught and tested slide floors (Playground 5 and 6), taught and twisted turnable arrows (Playground 7 and 8) and taught and tested rays through portals (Playground 9 and 10). None of them used a light barrier or a mirror box, and only Playground 2 and 4 used fizzlers.

- **Run 3, chamber 2: the corridor two boxes seal** (combine; sketched 2026-11-23, its run detailed in `docs/campaign.md` the same day). The door to the exit stands at the end of a one-tile corridor, linked by two buttons in a row along it, so it opens only while both are held (guideline 62); the room before the corridor holds two boxes. The catch: a box resting on a button in the corridor bars it (guideline 67), so whichever button gets its box first shuts the subject off from the other. Put down from the room's side, it shuts the subject out of the corridor; carried past and put down from beyond, as chamber 3.1 teaches, it shuts the second box out. It resolves by the order (the patterns' "Both orders fail": park one part-way): the first box is carried past both buttons and left on the tile beyond the far one; the second goes onto the far button, where the subject turns and puts it down on the near one; then the subject, still on the far button, picks the first box up from beyond, steps onto its tile, turns, puts it down on the far button and walks out. One key move, leaving a box beyond its button before the other goes down; every put-down around it is chamber 3.1's. The lure: chamber 3.1's answer for the first box, carried past a button and put down on it from beyond: on the far button it shuts the second box out, the door shut and the near button's antline unlit; on the near button, from the far one, the door opens while the subject stands there and shuts on the step off, the second box still out in the room. Filling the near button first from the room shuts the subject out, the far button's antline unlit. Each is undone by picking the box up again. What every route must do that no earlier route did, and that looks like no earlier move (guideline 113): leave a box beyond its button, then come back for it, the parking; two boxes at once, two antlines into one door and turning in place twice are only its setting. The risk: right after 3.1, it may read as 3.1 twice, so build 3.1 and this chamber so they don't look alike (3.1 needn't be a bare corridor, or this one's corridor can bend). Plants: 3.1 (idea 5 below, "Solved but trapped"): the turn key puts the box down behind, and a box on a button in a one-tile corridor shuts it; 1.3: a box holds a button. No chamber teaches the door several buttons link before this one (the run's detail, as the author chose), so its antlines teach it here: the first box down lights one line and the door stays shut. Keep it unrehearsed (guideline 47): 3.1 has one box and one button, so it never leaves a box beyond its button. A box bars its tile is offered in 3.1, to the wrong try that puts the box down from the near side (guideline 108), and forced here. Walls: every wall steel gives one key move and forces the turn key; walls that take portals in the room and over the tile beyond the far button give a second way past the boxes, a portal pair set from beyond and shot over the resting boxes, which also makes the turn key avoidable, since a portal's exit or a run-up faces the subject the way it needs. Prefer steel (guideline 11), unless the author's play of 3.1 asks for portals back. Handbook lesson to propose once the author has played it (it gives the move away): a resting box keeps the subject out and lets shots through, like the barriers guideline 74 lists, but the subject puts it there and can lift it, so it forces a portal only where no order of put-downs avoids the seal, and a free tile beyond a box's button often gives one. _Check:_ guideline 59 (a tile between the far button and the door); 62, 96 and 97 (an antline from each button: in a one-tile corridor the two lines would run on top of each other, so lead one through the walls, turning on wall tiles inside a wall, which takes walls thicker than the prototype's one-tile ones); 112 (no line under the parking tile, where the parked box would hide it at the key moment, as in cut 2.2); 40 (the door right in front of the exit, so its line runs out through it); 67 and 76 (every state finishable, a box in the corridor always picked up from a side the subject can reach); 56 (the room no larger than the order needs, with the boxes' starts clear of the start, since the prototype's cramped room added box shuffling to every route); 102 (a copy with one box removed comes back unsolvable). Feasibility, 2026-11-23: a throwaway prototype in a scratch folder, not a draft (9 × 5, the corridor along the top, the room below its first tile, every wall steel), came out at 13 moves and 0 portals, its route the one above; all 4,842 reachable states can be finished, and none without the turn key. ## Portals (basics)

1. **Gated islands** (teach or test). Three or four floor islands in a row, separated by lasers or open ditches, each with exactly one wall face that takes portals and sees the next island. The only way forward is a crossing per island, in order; a wrong shot is fixed by re-shooting. _Teaches:_ aiming from the right tile, and that either portal can be the entrance. _Check:_ guideline 78 (each island has a face it can shoot from to come back), guideline 72 (no island's face is the wall at the end of a laser run).
2. **Shoot past what you can't walk past** (laser teach). A corridor to the exit cut by a run of lasers; one wall beyond the run takes portals, one wall on the subject's side does too. _Teaches:_ lasers block walking, not shots. _Check:_ the wall beyond must not be the end-plate wall of the laser run (guideline 72).
3. **Hold the door with a shot** (door teach, or a twist after box-and-button chambers). A button opens a door, and there is no box. Standing on the button, the subject faces the open doorway and a wall beyond it that takes portals; it shoots, steps off (the door closes), shoots the second portal on its own side and crosses. Players easily miss this move and search for a box instead, so the cue must come from the layout: no box anywhere in the chamber, the button tile looking straight through the doorway at the wall that takes portals, an antline from the button to the door. Expect players to hunt for a box. _Teaches:_ open doors pass shots; a door closing doesn't close portals. _Check:_ the far wall must not be the tile right behind the door (those faces never take portals); after this chamber, every box-and-door chamber must decide whether this move is allowed (guideline 40).
4. **The missing rule** (light barrier teach by contrast, or laser twist). Two routes to the exit area: one cut by a light barrier run, one by a laser run, each with a wall beyond that takes portals. Shooting across the light barrier misses (the band reacts); across the laser it works. _Teaches:_ light barriers stop shots, lasers don't. _Check:_ the light barrier's own end-plate walls take no portals (guideline 72); give the light barrier no link, so it stays on.

## Boxes, buttons and doors

5. **Solved but trapped** (box twist). A one-tile corridor: start, box, floor, button, floor, floor, exit, where the button opens the exit. Carrying the box to the button and putting it down from the start side seals the corridor with the exit open beyond it; the answer is to carry the box over the button, turn on the far side (turn key) and put it down facing back. _Teaches:_ a box bars its tile; the put-down tile is the one faced. _Check:_ guideline 59 (button not next to the exit), guideline 67; it's fully reversible, which is why it suits a game with no undo.
6. **Hands full** (box test). The button sits in a room reached only by portal. Carrying the box, the subject can't shoot, so it must shoot first, or put the box down, shoot and pick it up again. Harder: the only tile that sees the right wall is one the subject reaches while carrying. _Check:_ guideline 79 (a tile to put the box down on wherever the subject can arrive carrying it).
7. **One box, two buttons in turn** (box combine). One box must hold button A (opens door A) and later button B (bridges a ditch or opens door B); taking it off A closes door A behind the subject, so the order and a portal placed while A was held decide the route. To make it hard, the route must also go back to the box after placing a portal while A is held (guideline 10). _Teaches:_ nothing new; it's a test of planning. _Check:_ guideline 65 (the box doesn't start on tiles where it could land under the subject), guideline 59.
8. **Counterintuitive first move** (box twist). The box starts beside its button, but putting it there first blocks the corridor to the only wall the subject needs to shoot. The answer carries it away, shoots, then comes back. _Check:_ guideline 67 (a reachable tile beside the box wherever it rests).
9. **Prepare, then trigger** (switch combine). The exit opens only by a switch whose links also send both boxes home, so the buttons they held come up and their doors close; the player must have placed portals beforehand so the route to the exit survives. The antlines show what the switch will do. _Check:_ guideline 68 (a switch, not a button, sends boxes home), guideline 64 (box start tiles), guideline 65.

## Fizzlers

10. **Set your portals first** (fizzler test). Box on one side of a fizzler that is on, button on the other. Walking the box through destroys it and closes both portals; the answer is to place both portals from a tile whose shot lines miss the field, then carry the box through the portals. _Check:_ guideline 73 (don't make the player stand in the field to aim), guideline 72 (the walls behind the connectors take no portals).
11. **Fizzle to respawn** (fizzler twist). The box starts on button B1, which holds door D1 open. The route needs the box elsewhere first (taking it closes D1), and later needs D1 open again from a side where B1 can't be reached (past an arrow). The answer: carry the box into the fizzler; it flies home onto B1 and D1 reopens. _Teaches:_ a destroyed box goes to its start, wherever that is. _Check:_ guideline 64 (its "box delivered by its start tile" used on purpose), guideline 65 (the subject is never on B1 when the box lands), an antline from B1 to D1 so the effect reads.
12. **Cheap first failure** (fizzler teach). The obvious walk carries the box straight into a fizzler that is on: it breaks up and lands back at its start two tiles away, and both portals close. Nothing is lost but a few steps, and the way round (a portal pair placed first) is then plain. A box can't _start_ on a fizzler, a door or a laser, so the demonstration has to be one the player causes. _Check:_ guideline 63 (the start tile stays in reach), guideline 73.
13. **A fizzler mid-slide** (fizzler and slide twist). A slide over a fizzler that is on closes both portals and breaks up a carried box, and the slide goes on. _Check:_ guideline 83.
14. **A mirror box carried around a fizzler** (fizzler and ray combine). _Check:_ guidelines 63 and 93.

## Light barriers

15. **A wall you can switch** (light barrier teach). A room split by a light barrier run held off by a button and its box; the barrier blocks walking and shots, so neither the subject nor a shot crosses until the box is on the button. _Check:_ guideline 59 (button not next to the band), guideline 60.
16. **Barrier as a stopper** (slide and light barrier combine). A slide lane overshoots the tile beside the exit; a light barrier across the lane, held off by a receiver lit by a ray, comes on when the player puts a box in the ray, and the next slide stops in front of the band. _Check:_ guideline 92 (the ray must not cross the barrier's tile), guideline 81 (work out every stop with the band on and off), guideline 93.

## Ditches and blocked walls

17. **Ferry across islands** (portal and box combine). Islands separated by open ditches, crossed only by portals; the box must reach the last island. Each hop is put down, re-aim, pick up, cross. _Check:_ guideline 79 (a tile to put the box down on each island), guideline 78 (a way back from each island while it matters). Chamber 1.4 is its one-hop form, the hop forced by islands that don't see each other (`chamber_4.md`). _Twist,_ from 1.4's cut variant with every wall plain: a box resting in front of a face refuses every crossing onto it, so a box parked on a landing tile shuts that way in; a part with a second face to land on lets the player recover and learn it, one with none is a dead end (guideline 67).
18. **Pull the rug** (ditch twist). A box rests on a bridged ditch, held by a button; releasing the button destroys the box, which flies home to a start tile beyond a barrier the subject can't carry it past. _Check:_ guideline 59 (never under the subject), guideline 64 (the start tile is meant to be reached this way).
19. **Steel that opens** (blocked wall teach). The only wall that sees across the gap is a blocked wall, which takes portals only while a button holds it; the box goes on the button, the portal goes on the wall, the subject crosses. _Teaches:_ blocked walls refuse portals until a link opens them. _Check:_ guideline 60 (no side of the linked blocked wall reachable by crossing from the button), guideline 73 (its portal vanishes if the wall is blocked again).
20. **The portal budget** (blocked wall combine). One button unblocks a group of walls, changing which faces take portals across the whole chamber, and the box that holds it is needed elsewhere too: lifting it blocks the walls again and closes every portal on them, so the player must choose which portals to place while it is held and which to leave on plain walls. A switch alone makes no budget: unblocking only adds faces, so using it at once costs nothing. _Check:_ guideline 73 (a portal on a blocked wall vanishes as the wall is blocked again), guideline 60 (no side of the linked walls reachable by crossing from the button), guideline 28 (show first that a vanished portal isn't lost).

## Slide floors (beyond Playground 5 and 6)

21. **Box as a stopper** (slide twist). A long lane ends at a dead end; the exit (or a side door) is one tile before the end. Without the box the subject overshoots; with the box on the end tile it stops beside the door. Harder: the only box is also needed on a button, so a portal has to take over one of its jobs. _Check:_ guidelines 81, 85 and 88 (free boxes make most of an open field reachable).
22. **A box carried across slides** (slide and box combine). _Check:_ guidelines 79, 81 and 83.
23. **Nine-square lattice** (slide and portal combine). A 3×3 lattice of plain stop tiles joined by slide lanes, some walled off; walls at lane ends take portals, so the player edits the graph. _Check:_ guideline 85 (every stop tile has a move that leads somewhere new), guideline 86 (no portal loop by surprise), guideline 87.

## Arrows and turnable arrows (beyond Playground 7 and 8)

24. **The arrow as a firing post** (arrow twist). The only tile that sees the wall the route needs is an arrow; the subject can face only the arrow's way there, so it has to turn the arrow with its button or switch to aim. _Check:_ guideline 77 for both ways the arrow points, guideline 89 (the player sees it turn first).
25. **Funnel, then flip** (arrow combine). A room entered through a fixed arrow; inside, a turnable arrow held by a button opens the way back only while held, so the box has to stay on the button, and the route must not need it afterwards. _Check:_ guideline 90 (reach the turnable arrow from plain floor), guideline 91, guideline 63 (no box needed beyond the valve).
26. **An arrow held by a box** (arrow and box combine). _Check:_ guidelines 77 and 91.
27. **Arrows changing where a slide ends** (arrow and slide combine). _Check:_ guidelines 81 and 90.

## Rays and mirror boxes (beyond Playground 9 and 10)

28. **Mirror box shown in place** (mirror box teach). The ray hits a mirror box resting one tile off, which turns it into a wall; moving the box one tile turns it onto the receiver. _Check:_ guideline 93 (every place the box can rest), guideline 80 (no switch where the subject stands to put it down).
29. **Mirror first, then needed elsewhere** (mirror box test). The mirror box lights the receiver that opens door 1, and then is needed on the button that opens door 2; the portal pair has to take over the ray. _Check:_ guidelines 93 and 92.
30. **Two jobs at the bend** (mirror box twist). The same as idea 29, except a button sits on the ray's corner tile: one mirror box can turn the ray and hold the button at once. Make another corner look like the obvious one. _Check:_ rays pass buttons and a resting mirror box presses one (`docs/elements.md`); keep only one box in the chamber.
31. **Toggle between two receivers** (mirror box twist). Without the mirror box the ray reaches receiver A (door 1); with it, receiver B (door 2). The route needs both doors in turn, so the box goes down, comes up and goes down again. _Check:_ guideline 92 for both rays, guideline 93.
32. **A portal keeps what a door gave briefly** (ray and door combine). While a button holds door D open, the only line to wall W runs through it: put the box on the button, shoot a portal onto W, take the box to a second button and let D close. A portal in the ray then sends it out beyond D into the receiver. _Check:_ W isn't the tile right behind D; guideline 92; what the subject itself can reach through the same portal pair (guideline 74).
33. **Relay with a detour** (ray twist). Receiver R1 switches on emitter E2, whose ray opens the exit through R2; the subject's only route crosses the first ray, which would darken the whole chain. The answer routes the first ray through a portal pair so it skips the route. _Check:_ guideline 92 (no ray crosses what its own receiver holds), guideline 62 (anything else linking the exit must be active too); the macro solver refuses chambers with receivers, so check by hand (guideline 101).
34. **A ray crossing the tile before its own door** (ray twist). _Check:_ guideline 92.
35. **A run of four** (mirror box sequence across 3–4 chambers). One mirror box turns the ray onto a receiver; then the corner tile is a button; then two receivers; then a chamber that looks like the third but needs the portal pair instead, because the mirror box is needed on a button out of line. Let an earlier chamber of the four use the portal pair to carry the ray where nothing depends on it, so the piece the last one turns on is already planted. _Check:_ each chamber on its own as above; the fourth is the one that breaks the habit.

## Combine across elements

36. **Filters** (combine, late). One chamber where the subject, the box, a shot and the ray each reach a different region: an open ditch passes rays and shots but not the subject; a fizzler passes the subject but not the box or a shot; a light barrier passes nothing. The puzzle is ferrying each across its own barrier. _Check:_ guideline 83 (if slides are involved), guideline 92, and the solver's refusal of chambers with receivers.
37. **The capstone** (finale). It introduces nothing new and combines nearly everything. The last chamber needs synthesis and spectacle: several elements working at once, a visible chain of doors with lit antlines, and a catch no harder than the run before it (guideline 50).
