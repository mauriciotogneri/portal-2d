# Chamber Elements

Reference for the elements a chamber file (`src/chambers/*.json`) can contain, carried over from the original game's help page with the light barrier and the blocked wall added in place of its barrier, and the slide floor, the arrow a link turns, the emitter, the receiver, the mirror box and the box that starts absent, which the original game didn't have, followed by the chamber file format, the rules and what adding an element touches.

| # | Element | Description | Can a link change it? | Do shots pass it? | Can the subject walk onto it? | Quantity |
|---|---------|-------------|-----------------------|-------------------|-------------------------------|----------|
| 1 | Subject | Where the subject starts | No | Its own shots leave from its tile, at any angle | — | 1 |
| 2 | Wall | A solid wall, which takes portals | No | No: a portal opens on it | No (only by crossing a portal on it) | 0..∞ |
| 3 | Blocked wall | A wall that refuses portals while blocked | Yes, a one-tile one | No: a portal opens on it only while unblocked | No (only by crossing a portal on it) | 0..∞ |
| 4 | Light barrier | A field of light, on a floor tile only | Yes | Only when off | Only when off | 0..∞ |
| 5 | Ditch | A hole in the floor, open or bridged | Yes | Yes | Only when bridged | 0..∞ |
| 6 | Box | Can be carried, and holds a button down; one can start absent, until a link brings it in, and one a link reaches is absent once destroyed, until a link brings it in again | Yes (sent back to its start, or brought in there) | Yes | No (it is picked up from beside it) | 0..∞ |
| 7 | Button | Active while the subject or a box is on it, acting on its links | No | Yes | Yes | 0..∞ |
| 8 | Switch | Active from its first use until the chamber is loaded again, acting on its links | No | Yes | Yes | 0..∞ |
| 9 | Door | Closed or open | Yes | Only when open | Only when open | 0..∞ |
| 10 | Fizzler | While on, closes both portals and destroys a carried box | Yes | Only when off | Yes | 0..∞ |
| 11 | Exit | Reaching it completes the chamber; closed or open | Yes | Only when open | Only when open | 1 |
| 12 | Laser | Keeps the subject out while on | Yes | Yes | Only when off | 0..∞ |
| 13 | Arrow | Lets the subject enter only its way; one with a turned way is turned by a link | Yes, one with a turned way | Yes | Only in its way | 0..∞ |
| 14 | Slide floor | A floor tile that keeps the subject moving the way it entered it | No | Yes | Yes, and slides on | 0..∞ |
| 15 | Emitter | A wall tile that sends a ray from one face while it is on | Yes | No: the shot misses | No | 0..∞ |
| 16 | Receiver | A wall tile, active while a ray reaches its listening face, acting on its links | No | No: the shot misses | No | 0..∞ |
| 17 | Mirror box | A box with a mirror, which turns a ray a quarter turn while it rests | Yes (sent back to its start) | Yes | No (it is picked up from beside it) | 0..∞ |

## Notes

- Buttons, switches and receivers act on elements through links, by id. An element a link reaches is unblocked while every button, switch and receiver linking it is active (a button held down, a switch used, a receiver lit), and blocked otherwise (see [blocking](#blocking) and [links](#links)).
- Unblocked, a blocked wall takes portals, a light barrier, a fizzler or a laser is off, a ditch is bridged, a door or the exit is open, an emitter is on, and an arrow with a turned way points its turned way; blocked, each is the other way. A box, a mirror box among them, is instead sent back to its start each time it is unblocked, or brought in there while it is absent.
- A button, switch or receiver can link many elements, and many of them can link one. For example, holding a button down can open a door and switch off a light barrier at the same time, and a door two buttons link opens only while both are held.

## Chamber format

A chamber file is a JSON object in format 2 (`ChamberData` in `src/game/chamber_data.ts`). Positions are tiles: `x` grows to the right and `y` upwards from the bottom row, both from 0. Every coordinate, size, id and count is a whole number. `version`, `size`, `subject` and `exit` are required; every list, `groups` and `par` may be left out, and a list left out means the chamber has none of that element. A field the format doesn't name is an error.

| # | Field | Holds |
|---|-------|-------|
| 1 | `version` | The format's version: `2`. |
| 2 | `size` | `width` and `height`, in tiles, each 1 or more. |
| 3 | `subject` | Where the subject starts: `x`, `y` and `direction` (`UP`, `DOWN`, `LEFT` or `RIGHT`), all required. |
| 4 | `exit` | The exit, a single entry: `x`, `y`, and optionally `id` and `blocked`. It stands in the chamber's outer wall, away from its corners. |
| 5 | `walls` | One tile (`x`, `y`) or a rectangle of tiles (`xFrom`, `xTo`, `yFrom`, `yTo`, both ends included, each from no greater than its to): walls, which take portals. |
| 6 | `blockedWalls` | One tile (`x`, `y`, and optionally `id` and `blocked`) or a rectangle of tiles (corners as for `walls`, and optionally `blocked`): blocked walls, which refuse portals while blocked. Only a one-tile entry can have an `id`, so only it can be linked. |
| 7 | `emitters` | `x`, `y` and `direction` (`UP`, `DOWN`, `LEFT` or `RIGHT`: the face it fires from), all required, and optionally `id` and `blocked`: emitters, wall tiles of their own that send a ray while they are on (see [rays](#rays)). An emitter's `blocked` is false when absent, so it starts on, unless a link reaches it: then it takes no `blocked` and starts off (see [blocking](#blocking)). |
| 8 | `receivers` | `x`, `y`, `direction` (`UP`, `DOWN`, `LEFT` or `RIGHT`: the face it listens on) and `links`, all required, the links as a button's: receivers, wall tiles of their own that act on their links while a ray reaches that face (see [receivers](#receivers)). |
| 9 | `slideFloors` | One tile (`x`, `y`) or a rectangle of tiles (corners as for `walls`): slide floors, which keep the subject moving (see [a slide](#a-slide)). They have no `id` or `blocked`, so links never reach them. |
| 10 | `ditches`, `doors`, `fizzlers` | `x`, `y`, and optionally `id` and `blocked`. |
| 11 | `lasers` | `x`, `y`, `direction` (`H` or `V`: how it is drawn and which walls end its run, which take no portal, and two lasers can share a tile only when they cross), and optionally `id` and `blocked`. |
| 12 | `lightBarriers` | `x`, `y`, `direction` (`H` or `V`: how it is drawn and which walls end its run, which take no portal), and optionally `id` and `blocked`. |
| 13 | `boxes` | `x`, `y`, and optionally `id`, `mirror` (`/` or `\`, written `"\\"` in JSON) and `absent`: a box with a `mirror` is a mirror box, its mirror from corner to corner, bottom left to top right for `/` and top left to bottom right for `\`, with y growing upwards (see [rays](#rays)); a box with `absent` true starts absent, on no tile, until a link brings it in on its start tile, `x` and `y` (see [a box that starts absent](#a-box-that-starts-absent)), and needs a button, switch or receiver that links it. |
| 14 | `buttons`, `switches` | `x`, `y` and `links`, all required: the ids of the elements and link groups it acts on (see [links](#links)). |
| 15 | `arrows` | `x`, `y` and `direction` (`UP`, `DOWN`, `LEFT` or `RIGHT`), all required: the one way the subject can enter the tile; and optionally `id` and `turnedDirection` (`UP`, `DOWN`, `LEFT` or `RIGHT`), always together, a quarter turn from `direction`: the way it points while a link holds it turned (see [links](#links)). An arrow takes no `blocked`: every one starts pointing its `direction`, so one meant to start turned swaps its two ways. |
| 16 | `groups` | Link groups, each with an `id` and `links`, both required: the ids of the elements it stands for, never another group. |
| 17 | `antlines` | Dotted lines on the floor, each with `link` and `path`, both required: `link` the id of the one element it leads to, never a group's, and `path` its corner tiles (`x`, `y`), at least two, from the tile of a button, switch or receiver that links that element to the element's own tile. |
| 18 | `par` | How well the chamber can be solved: optionally `moves` and `portals`, each 0 or more, the fewest moves and the fewest portals. Reserved: nothing writes or uses it yet (see `docs/solver.md`). |

- **`id`:** 1 or more, unique among every element and group, and needed only by what a button, switch, receiver or group links to; an element without one can't be linked.
- **`blocked`:** true when absent, but on an emitter, where it is false (see [blocking](#blocking)). An element a button, switch or receiver links, by its id or through a group, never has it, either way: it starts blocked, and those linking it decide when it is unblocked.
- **`antlines`:** only drawn, as in the original game, to show which button, switch or receiver acts on which element: they change nothing in the rules, the solver or the recorded solutions. A chamber may have none, and a link needs none; they are meant mostly for the tutorial and the early chambers. A line is lit while the button, switch or receiver on its first corner is active (a button held down, a switch used, a receiver lit), whatever the element it leads to does, a line to a box included, so of the lines to an element several of them link, the unlit ones show which are still to be made active. Each corner shares a row or a column with the one before it, so the line runs straight between them; it may cross walls, its dots drawn over the top of a plain wall or a blocked wall no link reaches but hidden under an emitter or a receiver, and run under any element. It may end on a blocked wall a link reaches, when that is the element it leads to, but never run through one, which hides it there in every state: its steel is painted over the line while it is blocked, and no dots are drawn on it while it isn't. A line from a receiver starts on the receiver's wall tile, hidden under it up to the floor. The chamber's hash, which a recorded solution keeps, leaves them out, so adding, moving or removing one needs no new recording. How they look: `docs/sprites.md`.
- **Other versions:** a file whose `version` is missing or not 2 is refused.

## Rules

The rules the game plays, in `src/game/rules/` (`step.ts`), pinned by each chamber's recorded solution and by the behaviour fixtures (`npm run replay:check`). They follow the original game's rules apart from the [changes from the original rules](#changes-from-the-original-rules) listed at the end, and add two elements the original game didn't have, in place of its barrier: the light barrier, which plays a barrier's rules on a floor tile (fixture: `light_barrier_switches_off_and_on`), and the blocked wall, which a link can switch between refusing and taking portals, as a barrier on a wall did (fixture: `blocked_wall_switches_off_and_on`). They also add one with no counterpart there, the slide floor, which keeps the subject moving (see [a slide](#a-slide); fixtures: every `slide_*` one, and `portal_onto_slide_floor_slides`), and let a link turn an arrow (see [links](#links); fixtures: every `arrow_*` one, `slide_stops_on_turned_arrow`, `slide_stops_before_turned_back_arrow`, `portal_onto_turned_arrow` and `portal_onto_turned_back_arrow_refused`). Three more pieces have no counterpart there either: the emitter, which sends a ray, the receiver, which acts on its links while a ray reaches it, and the mirror box, a box that turns a ray (see [rays](#rays) and [receivers](#receivers), where their fixtures are named).

Tiles are counted with x growing to the right and y growing upwards from the bottom row, so "up" is towards a higher y. Chambers the validator rejects (see [what the validator rejects](#what-the-validator-rejects)) are outside these rules.

### Blocking

Blocked walls, light barriers, ditches, doors, the exit, fizzlers and lasers each have one flag, `blocked` in the chamber file (true when absent). Blocked means: a blocked wall refuses portals, a ditch is open, a door or the exit is closed, a light barrier, a fizzler or a laser is on. An arrow with a turned way has the flag too, but no `blocked` field, so it always starts blocked: blocked, it points its own way (`direction`), unblocked, its turned way. Emitters have it as well, but false when absent: a blocked emitter is off, so an emitter no link reaches starts on unless its file says `blocked: true`. An element a button, switch or receiver links, by its id or through a group, starts blocked whatever its file says (an emitter off, an arrow pointing its own way), since the validator rejects a `blocked` on it (fixture, on a chamber read without the layout checks: `linked_door_starts_closed_whatever_its_file_says`), and the chamber's start settles it as those linking it allow. Buttons, switches and receivers change it through [links](#links); nothing else does.

### A step

The player gives one action per step: move or turn (up, down, left or right), shoot blue or orange (the way the subject faces, or aimed at any angle), or interact. A step carries out every consequence of its action before it ends; nothing waits for a later step.

1. The action runs (see [Actions](#actions)); a move onto slide floor goes on as [a slide](#a-slide), within this step.
2. A carried box moves onto the subject's tile.
3. Then, repeated until a pass changes nothing:
   1. the [fizzler rule](#the-fizzler-rule);
   2. every [button](#buttons), in the chamber's list order, with its links;
   3. every [receiver](#receivers), in the chamber's list order, with its links, each judged on the rays as they are after every change made before it;
   4. every [box lying where something blocks](#a-box-where-something-blocks), in the chamber's list order.

   It always ends. The subject doesn't move while a step settles, so a button changes only as a box moves, and boxes move only to their start tiles, at most once each in a step, but for a box a link reaches, which being destroyed takes off the floor and a link may then bring in again, at its start, where nothing destroys it; no switch is used while it settles, and a switch's used flag only ever turns on. An element a link reaches is unblocked exactly while every button, switch and receiver linking it is active, so one of them turning active (a button going down, a receiver lighting) can only unblock the elements it links, or leave them as they are, and one turning inactive (a button coming up, a receiver going dark) can only block them. Apart from boxes, every element a button going down or a receiver lighting unblocks lets more light through (a door or the exit opens, a light barrier switches off, an emitter switches on) or changes nothing a ray meets (a ditch, a laser or a fizzler switches off, a blocked wall takes portals, an arrow turns), and every element a button coming up or a receiver going dark blocks lets less through (a door or the exit closes, a light barrier switches on, an emitter switches off, a fizzler or a blocked wall closes a portal) or changes nothing a ray meets. So no receiver can put out its own light, and settling can't flip back and forth. A cap of 32 passes still guards against a mistake: a 32nd pass that still changes something is an error. That settling ends is argued here, not proven. The behaviour fixtures (a chain of receivers, each opening a door on the next one's ray, a relay through an emitter) and the random walk test it. Nothing in the game catches the error, so play would stop; a chamber that reaches the cap means revisiting this argument, not raising the cap.
4. The [exit check](#the-exit).

Once the chamber is completed, a step changes nothing.

A chamber starts from its file's state, every element a link reaches blocked (see [blocking](#blocking)), settled like the end of a step: with no action before them, the repeat loop runs until a pass changes nothing, then the exit check, and their events are reported. So a button under the subject or a box starts pressed, and a receiver a ray reaches starts lit, each with its links acted on, and every switch starts unused.

Events are reported in the order they happen. The rules keep no counters: the game counts a move for each step that puts the subject on another tile (a portal crossing included, and a slide as one, however many tiles it crosses) and a portal for each portal-opened event.

### Actions

#### Move or turn

On an arrow tile, the three directions other than the arrow's do nothing at all. An arrow's way, here and everywhere in these rules, is the way it points now, which a link can turn (see [links](#links)). Every other move or turn first faces the subject that way. A turn does only that, unless the subject already faced that way: then it moves like a move. The game takes a turn for every move key press; a move, which turns and steps at once, is played only by the recorded solutions, the behaviour fixtures and the solver. To move, it looks at the next tile in that direction:

- **Outside the chamber:** a [wall that takes no portals](#outside-the-chamber).
- **A wall tile** (a wall, blocked or not, an emitter or a receiver): if both portals are open and this wall holds one of them on the side facing the subject, the subject [crosses](#crossing-a-portal) that portal. Otherwise nothing more happens.
- **Any other tile:** the subject steps onto it if it can enter it. It can't enter a tile with a blocked light barrier, ditch, door, exit or laser (where two lasers cross, either one that is on keeps it out), nor a tile where a box rests, nor an arrow tile unless it moves the arrow's way. Fizzlers, buttons, switches and rays never stop it. Walking into something it can't enter leaves it facing that way, without a sound.

Of the tile the subject leaves, only an arrow counts (above); everything else there is ignored, and only the tile it moves onto is checked (see [something blocking under the subject](#something-blocking-under-the-subject), and a box sent back under it in [one box per tile](#one-box-per-tile)).

A subject that steps onto slide floor slides on (see [a slide](#a-slide)). Standing on slide floor changes nothing else: a turn only turns it, a shot leaves as from anywhere, and a move onto plain floor is one step.

#### Crossing a portal

The subject comes out on the tile in front of the other portal, facing away from that portal's wall. That tile is checked like a step onto it, in that facing, so an arrow there must point the way the subject comes out, no box may rest there, no ditch there may be open, and no light barrier or laser there may be on. If the subject can enter it, it is put there: `crossed`, and on slide floor it slides on from there, the way it came out (see [a slide](#a-slide)). If not, the crossing is refused: `refused`, and the subject stays in front of the portal it walked into, facing it. A carried box comes along. Two portals can face the same tile; walking from it into one of them brings the subject back onto it, facing the new way, if the check allows.

#### A slide

A subject that enters slide floor, by a move or out of a portal, keeps moving the way it faces, one tile at a time, within the same step:

- **A floor tile ahead** is checked like a step onto it (see [Move or turn](#move-or-turn)): an open ditch, a laser or a light barrier that is on, a closed door, the closed exit, an arrow pointing another way or a resting box stops the slide on the tile it is on. A tile it can enter, it enters: on slide floor it goes on; on any other tile, a bridged ditch, an open door, a switch, an arrow pointing its way and the open exit included, it stops there.
- **A wall ahead** stops it, unless the wall holds a portal on the side facing the subject while the other portal is open: then it crosses (see [crossing a portal](#crossing-a-portal)) and goes on the way it came out if it came out onto slide floor, or stops there if not. A crossing onto a tile the subject can't enter stops the slide in front of the entry portal, silently, where a crossing tried from rest is `refused`.
- **On each tile it passes**, the carried box comes along and the [fizzler rule](#the-fizzler-rule) runs: a fizzler that is on closes both portals and destroys a carried box, and the slide goes on; a wall whose portal closed is a plain wall for the rest of it. Buttons are judged only once the slide has ended, as the step goes on, so passing over a button doesn't press it, and stopping on one does. So are receivers: passing through a ray doesn't cut it, and stopping in one does (see [rays](#rays)).
- **A loop:** a slide that would enter a tile the same way it already entered it in this slide (going round through two portals; the tile it started from doesn't count) is refused whole: nothing changes but the subject's facing, which turns to the way pressed, as a refused crossing leaves it; `refused`, and nothing settles.

Loose boxes never slide; a box is put down on slide floor and picked up from it as on plain floor. A slide counts as one move, and as none when it ends back on the tile it started from; with the facing also unchanged, the step changes nothing, though its events are still reported. It reports `slideStarted` the first time the subject moves on from a slide floor tile it entered, and `slideStopped` when that slide ends; entering slide floor and stopping at once reports neither. The start plays a sound, once however long the slide runs; the stop plays none. The game draws a slide along its path, showing what happens on each tile as the subject gets there and the rest once it stops (see `docs/sprites.md`, "Steps").

#### Shoot blue or orange

A shot takes one of two forms. The **straight shot** leaves the middle of the subject's tile the way it faces; the recorded solutions, the behaviour fixtures and the solver play it. The **aimed shot**, which the game plays, leaves from a point of the subject's tile (kept a millionth of a tile inside its edges) at any angle, whatever way the subject faces, but on an arrow tile, where it is the straight shot the way the subject faces, which is the arrow's way (see [links](#links)). A straight shot is an aimed shot from the tile's middle along its row or column: the replay check plays both on every state of every recorded solution and behaviour fixture, and they must give the same step.

While the subject stands in a fizzler that is on, the portal keys do nothing, without a sound. While it carries a box, they are refused: `refused`, with the error sound and no ripple, since no shot leaves (it can't stand in a fizzler that is on while carrying one, so the two never meet). Otherwise the shot goes on in a straight line through the tiles it passes, in order; the subject's own tile never stops it.

- A fizzler or a light barrier that is on, a closed door or the closed exit stops it: a miss. Ditches, lasers, boxes, buttons, switches, arrows, slide floors and rays, in any state, and anything not blocked let it through.
- Reaching the edge of the chamber is a miss, and so is a shot that passes an open door or the open exit in the chamber's edge, which leaves the chamber.
- A shot passing exactly through a corner where four tiles meet (within a billionth of a tile) is a miss when the two tiles on either side of its line there both stop it, since it is squeezed between them, or when the tile across the corner does, since it enters that tile through its corner. Grazing the corner of one tile, it goes on. A straight shot never meets a corner.
- A wall takes the portal on the side the shot enters (a straight shot's: its side facing the subject), unless that side faces a door or the exit, open or closed, or a laser or a light barrier running towards the wall (the wall at the end of its run, behind its end plate; a wall alongside the run still takes one), on or off, or a fizzler whose field spans its gap towards the wall (behind its connector), on or off, or it already holds a portal, of either colour, on that side or on either neighbouring side; a portal on the far side doesn't stop it. A blocked wall takes it the same way only while it isn't blocked; while it is, the shot is a miss. An emitter or a receiver takes none, on any face: the shot is a miss.
- A fizzler's field spans its gap from the wall on its left to the one on its right, unless more of what lines a gap (a wall of any kind, a door, the exit, a fizzler, a light barrier or a ditch, in any state; outside the chamber lines none) lies above and below it than left and right, when it spans from top to bottom; it has a connector at each end of its span where no fizzler continues it, so a run of fizzlers side by side across a gap has one at each of its two ends. The wall at the end of its way through, where it has no connector, still takes a portal.

A hit opens the portal of that colour there, closing it wherever it was before: `bluePortalOpened` or `orangePortalOpened`. A miss: `refused` (the game plays the shot sound instead of the error sound, and draws a ripple where the shot stopped, see `docs/sprites.md`).

#### Interact

- On a switch: while carrying a box, `refused`, and the box stays carried. Otherwise the switch is used: `switchUsed`, and it is active from then on, until the chamber is loaded again (Restart chamber included); then it acts on its links (see [links](#links)). It can be used any number of times, and each use acts on its links again, so a linked box is sent back on each use while every other button, switch and receiver linking it is active. Standing on a switch, interact never picks up the box in front.
- Otherwise, while carrying a box: the box is put down on the tile the subject faces, `boxPutDown`, if a move that way could step onto that tile now (as below, for picking a box up from there), no fizzler there is on, no box rests there, since a tile holds [one box](#one-box-per-tile), and it holds neither the exit, where nothing would destroy a box when it closes, nor a switch, which the box would hide. Otherwise `refused`: a box is never put down on the subject's own tile.
- Otherwise, facing a tile where a box rests: the subject picks it up from where it stands, `boxPickedUp`, if a move that way could step onto that tile now but for the box (see [Move or turn](#move-or-turn): an arrow there must point that way, and nothing else there may block walking) and no fizzler there is on. The carried box then sits on the subject's tile, so a button the box held is released in the same step. The subject never picks up a box under it (see [one box per tile](#one-box-per-tile)).
- Otherwise nothing happens.

A box picked up while the subject stands in a fizzler that is on, from the tile it faces, is destroyed in the same step (see [the fizzler rule](#the-fizzler-rule)).

### The fizzler rule

If the subject stands in a fizzler that is on, both portals close and a carried box is destroyed (a slide also runs this rule on each tile it passes, see [a slide](#a-slide)): it goes back to its start tile (see [one box per tile](#one-box-per-tile)), or, if a link reaches it, is absent (see [a destroyed box a link reaches](#a-destroyed-box-a-link-reaches)), and is no longer carried, `carriedBoxDestroyed`. `fizzled` is reported at most once per step, and only if the subject wasn't standing in a fizzler that was on when the step began: it walked or crossed into the field, or the field turned on under it. Nothing remembers having been inside a field.

### A box where something blocks

A box lying on a tile where a door is closed, a ditch is open, or a laser, a light barrier or a fizzler is on is destroyed: it goes back to its start tile (see [one box per tile](#one-box-per-tile)), or, if a link reaches it, is absent (see [a destroyed box a link reaches](#a-destroyed-box-a-link-reaches)), `boxDestroyed`. That happens when the element closes or switches on under it; a box is never put down where one already has (see [Interact](#interact)). A carried box is left to the [fizzler rule](#the-fizzler-rule). The exit is left out, since no box can lie on it: the subject reaching it completes the chamber. A box's start is plain floor, slide floor or a button, so each box is destroyed at most once in a step. The game shows a box falling into a ditch still open at the end of the step as it gathers again at its start, and anywhere else coming apart and flying home in pieces, and a box a link reaches only falling, or breaking up where it was, all with the box-destroyed sound.

### One box per tile

A tile holds at most one box. The carried box, which sits on the subject's tile, doesn't count, but a resting box keeps the subject off its tile (see [Move or turn](#move-or-turn)), and no box is put down where another rests (see [Interact](#interact)). A box sent back to its start tile, destroyed by a fizzler or where something blocks, or sent back by a link, sends the box resting there, if any, back to its own start the same way, and so on; so does a box a link brings in. A destroyed box a link reaches lies nowhere, so it sends nothing on. Each box moves at most once, so the chain ends. Nothing reports it.

A box sent back to its start tile in any of these ways, or brought in there, while the subject stands there lands under the subject. The subject can walk off it but not back on, and picks it up only from beside it, facing it. This is the only way the subject and a resting box share a tile.

### A box that starts absent

A box with `absent` true starts absent: it lies on no tile and is in no hands, so the subject walks over its start tile, it holds no button down, stops no ray, takes no tile from a box put down, and nothing destroys it (fixtures: `absent_box_blocks_nothing`, `absent_box_presses_no_button`, `absent_box_stops_no_ray`). The first time a link sends it home, as a button going down, a switch used or a receiver lighting does once every button, switch and receiver linking it is active (see [links](#links)), it is brought in on its start tile instead, `boxAppeared`, sending a box resting there on to its own start and landing under the subject standing there (see [one box per tile](#one-box-per-tile)), and from then on it is a box like any other: a later link sends it home, `boxSentBack` (fixtures: `switch_brings_absent_box_in`, `button_brings_absent_box_in`, `receiver_brings_absent_box_in`, `absent_box_appears_onto_box`, `absent_box_appears_under_subject`, `brought_in_box_sent_home_again`). Once in, it is absent again only when destroyed, as any box a link reaches (see [a destroyed box a link reaches](#a-destroyed-box-a-link-reaches)). The game draws nothing where it will appear: it takes shape there from pieces gathering, as a box that fell into a ditch does (see `docs/sprites.md`, "Fly home"), with the sound of a box sent home. A mirror box can start absent too.

### A destroyed box a link reaches

A box that a button, switch or receiver links, by its id or through a group, doesn't go back to its start when it is destroyed, by [the fizzler rule](#the-fizzler-rule) or [where something blocks](#a-box-where-something-blocks): it is absent, as a box that starts absent is (above), on no tile and in no hands, sending no box on from its start, until a link next sends it home, which brings it in on its start tile, `boxAppeared`. A box no link reaches goes back to its start. A link sends a box home only as the last button, switch or receiver linking it turns active, or as a switch linking it is used again while they all are (see [links](#links)), so a box destroyed while a button or a receiver linking it is already active comes back only once that one turns inactive and active again. A chamber with such a box keeps its link within reach from wherever the box can be lost (`docs/level_design.md`). The game draws it falling into a ditch, or breaking up where it was as a fizzled portal does, and nothing at its start (see `docs/sprites.md`, "Fly home"), with the box-destroyed sound (fixtures: `linked_box_breaks_up_in_closing_door`, `linked_box_falls_into_opening_ditch`, `linked_box_carried_into_fizzler_stays_absent`, `linked_box_reached_from_fizzler_destroyed`, `fizzled_linked_box_brought_back`).

### Rays

An emitter or a receiver is a wall tile of its own: wherever these rules speak of a wall, it counts as one, stopping walking, shots and rays and joining the walls around it, but neither takes a portal on any face, so a shot at one misses whichever face it meets (fixture: `shots_at_devices_miss`).

An emitter that is on sends a ray from its firing face. A ray isn't stored: the rules trace every ray from the state whenever settling judges a receiver (`traceRays` in `src/game/rules/rays.ts`), and the game draws them from the state it shows. Each starts on the floor tile in front of its emitter, travelling away from it, and goes on tile by tile:

- **A floor tile** stops it, on that tile, while the subject stands there (with a carried box), a box with no mirror rests there, a light barrier there is on, or a door or the exit there is closed. A resting mirror box turns it (below), and it goes on from that tile. Everything else lets it through, in any state: ditches, fizzlers, lasers, buttons, switches, arrows and slide floors (fixtures: `ray_lights_receiver_from_start`, `ray_passes_floor_elements`, `switch_switches_emitter_on`, `ray_stopped_by_door_exit_barrier`).
- **A wall tile** (a wall, a blocked wall in any state, an emitter, a receiver, or outside the chamber's edge) stops it at the face it meets, unless that face is a receiver's listening face, which lights that [receiver](#receivers), or holds a portal while the other portal is open: then the ray goes on from the tile in front of the other portal, travelling away from that portal's wall (fixtures: `ray_through_portals`, `ray_into_lone_portal_stops`).

A mirror box plays every box rule (carried, picked up, put down, holding a button down, sent back by a link, destroyed where something blocks), and its mirror stays the same wherever it goes, through portals too. Resting, it turns a ray entering its tile, from any side, a quarter turn: with y growing upwards, `/` turns a ray going right to up, up to right, left to down and down to left; `\` turns right to down, down to right, left to up and up to left. Carried, it is on the subject's tile, where the subject stops the ray first (fixtures: `mirror_boxes_turn_rays`, `mirror_box_through_portal`, `fizzled_mirror_box_turns_ray_again`). The rules report nothing when a ray starts or stops turning at a mirror box; the game works out when one starts, from the rays before and after a step, for a sound (see `docs/interface.md`, "Sounds").

A ray blocks nothing and harms nothing: the subject walks into, stands in and walks through one, shots pass it, a box in one isn't destroyed, and rays cross each other freely.

Rays count only while a step settles and at a chamber's start, since only settling judges receivers. A slide passing through a ray doesn't cut it, and one that stops in it does. Walking into a ray, or coming out of a portal into it, darkens what it lit within that step, and walking out lights it again. A shot that opens or moves a portal routes rays through it within that step, and a portal a fizzler or a blocked wall closes no longer passes them. Putting a box down in a ray cuts it there (a mirror box turns it instead), and picking the box up lets the ray on past that tile (fixtures: `subject_cuts_ray`, `subject_leaves_ray`, `box_put_down_cuts_ray`, `box_picked_up_from_ray`, `slide_through_ray_changes_nothing`, `slide_stopping_in_ray_cuts_it`). A door, the exit or a light barrier that a receiver holds open or off while that receiver's own ray crosses it closes or switches on as soon as anything cuts the ray before it, and then stops the ray itself, so it stays that way once the cut ends. Like every linked element, it starts closed or on, so it opens or switches off only once a ray reaches the receiver some other way first, as a second emitter's ray turned by a mirror box does in the fixture (fixture: `ray_held_door_closes_behind_subject`).

A ray never enters a tile the same way twice, and two rays never reach one receiver at once. A ray can enter a tile a given way from one place only, fixed by the state: from the tile behind it (and only from one side of it, when a mirror box rests there) or, when that tile is a wall, out of the portal on its face; and nothing leads back onto the tile in front of an emitter, which takes no portal. So a ray's path never repeats, and two rays reaching one receiver would share every tile back to one emitter. The rules still stop a ray that would enter a tile the same way twice, a guard that never acts; a receiver two emitters can light is lit by one at a time (fixtures: `receiver_lit_by_either_emitter`, `receiver_dark_when_neither_reaches`).

### Buttons

A button is held down while the subject or any box is on its tile. Each pass judges the buttons in the chamber's list order, and each one acts on its links at once, so a later button sees what an earlier one did. Going down: `buttonPressed`; coming up: `buttonReleased`; either way it then acts on its links in order: each element it links is unblocked if every button, switch and receiver linking it is active, and blocked if not (see [links](#links)). The list order doesn't change how an element ends, since each ends as every button, switch and receiver linking it leaves it: the order sets only the order of the events, and can matter for what happens while an element is unblocked part way through a pass, such as a linked box sent home, and for what that sets off.

### Receivers

A receiver is lit while a ray reaches its listening face (see [rays](#rays)), and acts as a button held down while it is lit. Each pass judges the receivers after the buttons, in the chamber's list order, and each one acts on its links at once. Lighting: `receiverLit`; going dark: `receiverUnlit`; either way it then acts on its links in order, as a button does. Each plays a sound of its own, one for lighting and one for going dark. Its lit flag is kept in the state, as a button's pressed flag is, so it acts only when that flag changes, and settling always leaves the flag matching the rays.

Each receiver is judged on the rays as they are after every change made before it in the step, not on one tracing per pass, so a receiver listed after another sees in the same pass what that one's links changed: a button opening a door lights the receiver behind it, which opens a second door, whose ray lights a second receiver, which opens the exit, all in the step the button goes down, and the button coming up darkens the whole chain in its step (fixtures: `ray_lights_receiver_from_start`, `receivers_chain_open_exit`, `receivers_chain_let_go_together`). A receiver linked to an emitter relays: lighting switches the emitter on, whose ray can light another receiver within the same step, and going dark switches it off (fixtures: `receiver_relays_to_emitter`, `cut_relay_darkens_both`; an emitter on a button or a switch instead: `button_switches_emitter_on`, `button_switches_emitter_off`, `switch_switches_emitter_on`). When a receiver and a button, a switch or another receiver link one element, it is unblocked only while all of them are active, as for two buttons (see [links](#links)). A door a button and a lit receiver link starts closed, opens once a box carried onto the button holds it, closes when that box, put down in the ray, darkens the receiver, though the button stays held, and opens again when the box is picked up; the button going down again while the ray is cut leaves it closed (fixtures: `receiver_alone_leaves_door_closed`, `box_in_ray_closes_button_held_door`, `box_out_of_ray_reopens_door`, `button_pressed_while_ray_cut_leaves_door_closed`). A door a switch and a receiver link stays closed when the switch is used while the subject stands in the ray, opens as the subject steps out of it, with no other use of the switch, and closes as it steps back in (fixtures: `switch_used_in_ray_leaves_door_closed`, `leaving_ray_opens_switch_held_door`, `stepping_into_ray_closes_switch_held_door`).

### Links

A button, switch or receiver names its links by element id. Only blocked walls (one tile each), light barriers, ditches, boxes, fizzlers, doors, lasers, the exit, arrows with a turned way and emitters can be linked. A link can also name a link group, which stands for its elements: they act at the group's place in the list, in the group's order.

An element a link reaches is unblocked exactly while every button, switch and receiver linking it, by its id or through a group, is active: a button while it is held down, a receiver while it is lit, and a switch from its first use until the chamber is loaded again (the state keeps a used flag for each switch). One that links it twice, by its id and through a group, counts once. So a door two buttons link opens only once both are held, and closes when either comes up (fixtures: `door_opens_once_both_buttons_held`, `door_closes_when_either_button_released`), and a door a switch and a button link opens only while the button is held once the switch has been used, again each time the button goes down (fixtures: `switch_linked_door_stays_closed_on_button`, `button_release_closes_switch_linked_door`, `button_reopens_door_without_switch`). Nothing opens an element while only some of those linking it are active, so "either of two buttons opens it" takes two elements, such as two doors on two routes. Whenever a button goes down or comes up, a receiver lights or goes dark, or a switch is used, it acts on each element it links, in order: the unblocked column below if every button, switch and receiver linking that element is now active, the blocked column if not. Either changes nothing on an element already that way, but a box, which goes back to its start each time it is unblocked: when a change leaves every one linking it active, and on each further use of a linked switch while they all are (fixtures: `box_stays_with_one_of_two_links_active`, `box_goes_home_once_both_links_active`, `switch_sends_box_back_each_use`). Every element a link reaches starts blocked, and the chamber's start settles it (see [blocking](#blocking)), so none is ever out of step with those linking it.

| # | Element | Unblocked (every button, switch and receiver linking it active: held down, used, lit) | Blocked (any of them not active) |
|---|---------|-------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------|
| 1 | Blocked wall | Takes portals: `blockedWallSwitched` | Refuses portals, and a portal on any of its sides closes: `blockedWallSwitched` |
| 2 | Light barrier | Off: `lightBarrierSwitchedOff` | On: `lightBarrierSwitchedOn` |
| 3 | Ditch | Bridged: `ditchSwitched` | Open: `ditchSwitched` |
| 4 | Door | Opened: `doorOpened` | Closed: `doorClosed` |
| 5 | Exit | Opened: `exitOpened` | Closed: `exitClosed` |
| 6 | Fizzler | Off: `fizzlerSwitchedOff` | On: `fizzlerSwitchedOn` (see [the fizzler rule](#the-fizzler-rule)), and a portal on a wall side facing it closes: `portalFizzled` |
| 7 | Laser | Off: `laserSwitchedOff` | On: `laserSwitchedOn` |
| 8 | Box | Back to its start tile (see [one box per tile](#one-box-per-tile)), unless it is carried: `boxSentBack`, when it was away from there; brought in there, if it is absent, having started absent or been destroyed: `boxAppeared` (see [a box that starts absent](#a-box-that-starts-absent) and [a destroyed box a link reaches](#a-destroyed-box-a-link-reaches)) | Nothing; the validator warns about a box linked to a button or a receiver |
| 9 | Arrow | Points its turned way: `arrowTurned`; a subject on it turns with it | Points its own way: `arrowTurned`; a subject on it turns with it |
| 10 | Emitter | On: `emitterSwitchedOn`, and its ray leaves its firing face (see [rays](#rays)) | Off: `emitterSwitchedOff`, and it sends no ray |

A door or the exit reports its event only when it actually opens or closes, an arrow `arrowTurned` only when it actually turns, a blocked wall `blockedWallSwitched` and a ditch `ditchSwitched` only when it actually switches, a light barrier `lightBarrierSwitchedOff` or `lightBarrierSwitchedOn`, a laser `laserSwitchedOff` or `laserSwitchedOn`, a fizzler `fizzlerSwitchedOff` or `fizzlerSwitchedOn` and an emitter `emitterSwitchedOff` or `emitterSwitchedOn` only when it actually switches off or on, a fizzler reports `portalFizzled` only when it closes a portal, and a box `boxSentBack` only when it was away from its start, or `boxAppeared` when it was absent; the others are silent.

An arrow turning under the subject turns the subject with it, to face the arrow's new way, so on an arrow the subject always faces the way it points, and its shots fly that way, aimed ones included; a carried box stays carried. It can then leave only that way. An arrow turns only as links act: as a button goes down or comes up, or a receiver lights or goes dark, while the step settles (a chamber's start included), or on a switch's use. A slide judges no button or receiver until it ends, so no arrow turns mid-slide, but a slide's end can turn one, under the subject too.

A blocked wall is a wall in every state: it stops walking and shots, and joins the walls around it. Blocking it closes a portal already on it, silently. A fizzler switching on closes a portal on a wall side facing its tile, one opened while it was off (a fizzler that is on stops shots and keeps the subject from shooting), which only the wall at the end of its way through can hold, since the walls behind its connectors take none: `portalFizzled`, which the game shows by breaking the portal up, with no sound. A light barrier stands only on a floor tile, which never holds a portal, so switching it on closes none.

### The exit

A step that ends with the subject on the exit completes the chamber, `exitReached`. The subject can only get there while the exit is open, since walking and crossing a portal both refuse a closed exit, so the chamber is completed even when the same step closes the exit under it.

### Something blocking under the subject

A door or the exit closing, a ditch opening, or a light barrier or a laser turning on under the subject does nothing to it: it can still turn, shoot, interact and walk off. The exit, though, closes under it only in the step that brings it there, which completes the chamber all the same (see [the exit](#the-exit)), and after that nothing changes. A box it carries is kept, and no box lies there with it, since a box shares the subject's tile only on the box's start (see [one box per tile](#one-box-per-tile)), which is plain floor, slide floor or a button. A fizzler turning on under the subject runs the fizzler rule in that same step. A receiver can do this through its own ray: stepping onto an open door, a bridged ditch, or a laser or a light barrier that is off, which a receiver holds so while its own ray crosses that tile, cuts the ray and switches the tile under the subject (see [rays](#rays)). Chambers avoid this state (`docs/level_design.md`). An arrow turning under the subject turns it with it (see [links](#links)), which chambers may use.

### Outside the chamber

Outside the chamber's edge counts as a wall that takes no portals, and the validator requires enclosed chambers.

### What the validator rejects

A chamber file is read field by field (`chamber_parser.ts` in `src/game/rules/`), then checked as a whole (`validate_chamber.ts`), the two run by `load_chamber.ts`, and by `npm run chamber:check` on every chamber. Each problem names its field and its tile, where it has them. Errors keep a chamber from loading:

- **Fields:** a file that isn't a JSON object, a missing field, an unknown or misspelled one (an `id` on a blocked wall rectangle among them), a value of the wrong kind or out of range (a box's `mirror` other than `/` or `\` among them; see the [chamber format](#chamber-format)), a wall, blocked wall or slide floor rectangle whose from corner is past its to corner, an antline path with fewer than two corners, or a `version` other than 2.
- **Ids and links:** an id used twice (elements, arrows with a turned way and emitters among them, and groups share the ids), a link naming an id nothing has, a receiver's links included, and a group naming another group.
- **Linked elements:** a `blocked` field, true or false, on an element a button, switch or receiver links, by its id or through a group, since a linked element starts blocked and those linking it decide when it is unblocked; each such element is reported once.
- **Absent boxes:** a box that starts absent but that no button, switch or receiver links, by its id or through a group, since only a link brings it in.
- **Arrows:** an arrow with a turned way but no `id`, or an `id` but no turned way, and a turned way that is the arrow's own way or opposite it (a half turn), so every turn is a quarter turn.
- **Emitters and receivers:** an emitter whose firing face, or a receiver whose listening face, looks outside the chamber or onto a wall tile, since it fires onto, or listens on, a floor tile inside the chamber.
- **Antlines:** an antline leading to an id nothing has, or to a group, since an antline leads to one element; a corner outside the chamber, the same as the corner before it, or sharing neither a row nor a column with it (only the first such corner is reported); a first corner on no button, switch or receiver that links the element, by its id or through a group; a tile it runs through, between its first corner and its last, holding a blocked wall a link reaches, by its id or through a group, which would hide the line there in every state (each such tile is reported; the line may end on one); and a last corner away from the element's tile.
- **Bounds:** anything outside the chamber, the same wall tile listed twice, by walls, blocked walls, emitters or receivers, and the same slide floor tile listed twice.
- **What shares a tile:** a wall tile (a wall, a blocked wall, an emitter or a receiver) holds nothing else, so the subject, the exit and a light barrier are never inside a wall. A floor tile holds at most one of a light barrier, ditch, door, exit, fizzler, arrow, button, switch or laser, except two lasers that cross (one `H`, one `V`). A slide floor lies under what shares its tile, which can be a button, a fizzler, a laser, a light barrier, a box's start or the subject's start, but never a ditch, a door, the exit, an arrow or a switch. A box and the subject start on plain floor, slide floor or a button, with at most one box per tile, and the subject never on a box, since a box keeps it off its tile.
- **The edge:** every tile on the chamber's edge holds a wall, a blocked wall, an emitter, a receiver, a door or the exit, so the chamber is enclosed, and no slide floor stands there. The exit stands on the edge, so it leads out of the chamber, and not on a corner, where two sides meet.

One warning doesn't keep a chamber from loading: a button or a receiver linked to a box, since releasing the button, or the receiver going dark, does nothing to the box.

### Changes from the original rules

Every way these rules differ from the original game's, which this game played until stage 01 replaced them, each with its short id and the behaviour fixtures that pin it (`scripts/fixtures/fixtures.ts`). These rules take one action per step, finish it before the next and change nothing once the chamber is completed.

1. **`portal_exit_checked`** — Coming out of a portal onto a tile the subject couldn't walk onto is refused, with the error sound. The original rules ignored every walking check there, so the subject could stand on an open ditch, in a laser that is on, on a closed door or a wrong-way arrow. Fixtures: `portal_onto_open_ditch_refused`, `portal_onto_active_laser_refused`.
2. **`closed_exit_by_portal`** — A portal never brings the subject onto the closed exit, since no portal opens on a wall side facing the exit (`no_portal_beside_door`). The original rules let a portal bring it there and completed the chamber. Fixture: `no_portal_beside_door_or_exit`.
3. **`box_link_release`** — Releasing a button linked to a box does nothing to the box. The original rules stopped the game with an error. Fixture: `box_linked_button_release_does_nothing`.
4. **`fizzler_without_flag`** — The fizzler rule runs every step, and nothing remembers having been inside a field. The original rules counted the subject as inside a field until it stood on a tile with no fizzler at all, a fizzler that is off included, and fizzled nothing while it was inside. Fixture: `fizzler_rule_every_step`.
5. **`no_late_effects`** — Everything a step causes happens within it, in the step's order. In the original rules some effects landed a frame late (the button a carried box left, a fizzler the subject came out of a portal into or that turned on under it, the exit's completion, each link in a chain), so a key pressed in that frame acted first. Fixtures: `carried_box_off_button_closes_door_at_once`, `portal_onto_fizzler_fizzles_at_once`.
6. **`one_action_per_step`** — One action per step, in the order pressed. In the original rules a frame could carry several keys, run as a move, then a shot, then interact. No fixture: a step takes exactly one action.
7. **`carried_box_link`** — A link to a carried box does nothing. In the original rules the box went to its start tile for the rest of that frame, where it could hold a button down, then returned to the subject. Fixture: `carried_box_link_does_nothing`.
8. **`edge_is_wall`** — Outside the chamber's edge is wall that takes no portals. The original rules never checked the edge when walking, and a shot checked it only before each tile. Fixture: `edge_is_wall`.
9. **`completed_is_final`** — Once the chamber is completed, a step changes nothing. The original rules kept applying keys after completion. No fixture: the replay check plays every action on each recorded solution's completed state, and none may change it or report anything.
10. **`every_laser_blocks`** — Two lasers crossing on a tile keep the subject out while either of them is on. In the original rules only the first one listed there counted. Fixture: `crossing_lasers_either_blocks`.
11. **`no_shot_while_carrying`** — The portal keys don't shoot while the subject carries a box: `refused`, with the error sound. The original rules let it shoot with a box in hand. Putting the box down, shooting and picking it up again costs no extra move, except on a switch, or where no tile the subject can face can take the box; since the box goes down only on the tile faced, a shot at a wall takes turning away to put it down first and back to shoot, more presses but no extra move. Fixture: `no_shot_while_carrying`.
12. **`one_box_per_tile`** — A tile holds at most one box, the carried one aside: putting a box down on the tile faced where another rests is refused, with the error sound, and a box sent back to its start tile sends the box resting there back to its own start. The original rules let any number of boxes share a tile, through either. Fixtures: `no_box_put_down_on_a_box`, `box_sent_home_sends_box_home`.
13. **`no_portal_beside_door`** — A wall side facing a door or the exit never takes a portal, open or closed: a shot there misses. The original rules let one open there whenever a shot reached it, so a portal could bring the subject into a doorway or onto the exit. Fixture: `no_portal_beside_door_or_exit`.
14. **`fizzler_closes_facing_portal`** — A fizzler switching on closes a portal on a wall side facing its tile: `portalFizzled`. Only a wall at the end of its way through can hold one (`no_portal_behind_fizzler_connector`). So the subject comes out of a portal into a field that is on only when the crossing itself switches the field on, such as by leaving the button that held it off. The original rules kept the portal, so the subject could come out of it into the field that is on. Fixture: `fizzler_on_closes_portal_facing_it`.
15. **`no_portal_behind_end_plate`** — The wall at the end of a run of lasers or light barriers, behind its end plate, never takes a portal, on or off: a shot there misses. A wall alongside the run still takes one. The original rules let one open there whenever a shot reached it, drawn over the plate. Fixture: `no_portal_behind_end_plate`.
16. **`box_in_reach`** — Interact picks up a box on the tile the subject faces (the only place it picks one up, by `box_blocks_its_tile`), when it has nothing in its hands and no switch underfoot, if a move that way could step onto that tile but for the box and no fizzler there is on; otherwise nothing happens, silently. The original rules picked up only a box on the subject's own tile. Fixtures: `box_picked_up_from_faced_tile`, `box_out_of_reach`, `box_reached_from_fizzler_destroyed`.
17. **`box_destroyed_where_blocked`** — A box lying where a door closes, a ditch opens, or a laser, a light barrier or a fizzler switches on goes back to its start tile, `boxDestroyed`, or, one a link reaches, is absent (`linked_box_stays_destroyed`); no box is put down where one of them already blocks. The original rules left it there, out of reach until the element opened again. Fixtures: `box_breaks_up_in_closing_door`, `box_falls_into_opening_ditch`, `box_breaks_up_in_laser_turning_on`, `box_breaks_up_in_light_barrier_turning_on`, `box_breaks_up_in_fizzler_turning_on`, `box_not_put_down_facing_open_ditch`.
18. **`box_put_down_in_front`** — Interact puts a carried box down on the tile the subject faces, when that tile can take it (a move that way could step onto it, no fizzler there is on, no box rests there, and it holds neither the exit nor a switch), and otherwise refuses it, with the error sound, never putting it on the subject's own tile (`box_blocks_its_tile`). The original rules put it down only on the subject's own tile. Fixtures: `box_put_down_in_front`, `box_not_put_down_facing_exit`, `box_not_put_down_facing_switch`, `box_not_put_down_facing_a_box`, `box_not_put_down_facing_fizzler`, `box_kept_on_switch`.
19. **`box_blocks_its_tile`** — A resting box keeps the subject off its tile: walking into it only turns the subject that way, without a sound, and a crossing that would come out on it is refused, with the error sound; the carried box doesn't count, and shots still pass boxes. Boxes are picked up and put down only on the tile the subject faces, never on its own, so the subject shares a tile with a resting box only when that box is sent back to its start under it. The original rules let the subject walk onto boxes, and picked up and put down a box only on its own tile. Fixtures: `box_blocks_walking`, `portal_onto_box_refused`, `box_sent_home_under_subject`, `box_not_put_down_facing_wall`.
20. **`no_portal_behind_fizzler_connector`** — The walls a fizzler's field spans its gap between, behind its connectors, never take a portal, on or off: a shot there misses. The wall at the end of its way through still takes one. The original rules let one open there whenever a shot reached it, and the fizzler had no connectors. Fixture: `no_portal_behind_fizzler_connector`.
21. **`links_need_every_activator`** — An element a link reaches is unblocked exactly while every button, switch and receiver linking it is active: a button held down, a receiver lit, a switch used, which a switch stays from its first use until the chamber is loaded again (the state keeps a used flag for each switch, after the receivers' lit flags). Every element a link reaches starts blocked, whatever its file says, and the validator rejects a `blocked` on one. The original rules let the latest change win, whatever else held the element, so two buttons on one door undid each other. Fixtures: `door_opens_once_both_buttons_held`, `door_closes_when_either_button_released`, `switch_linked_door_stays_closed_on_button`, `button_release_closes_switch_linked_door`, `button_reopens_door_without_switch`, `receiver_alone_leaves_door_closed`, `box_in_ray_closes_button_held_door`, `box_out_of_ray_reopens_door`, `button_pressed_while_ray_cut_leaves_door_closed`, `switch_used_in_ray_leaves_door_closed`, `leaving_ray_opens_switch_held_door`, `stepping_into_ray_closes_switch_held_door`, `box_stays_with_one_of_two_links_active`, `box_goes_home_once_both_links_active`, `linked_door_starts_closed_whatever_its_file_says`.
22. **`linked_box_stays_destroyed`** — A destroyed box that a button, switch or receiver links is absent until a link brings it in on its start tile, `boxAppeared`, and sends no box on from there; a box no link reaches goes back to its start. ## Interactions

Every element against every other. Each cell names the rules below that bring the two together: what one does to the other, on the same tile or beside it, and what the validator forbids (the V rules), a case that can't happen in a chamber that loads. `·` would mean that nothing does, which holds for no pair today, and `—` that a chamber holds only one of it. The portals and the carried box have rows of their own, and the box's row is a box resting on the floor. The mirror box's row is a resting mirror box, which plays every box rule too, so it is the box's row with its mirror's rules added; carried, a mirror box is the carried box. The ray's row is what each element does to a ray, on its tile or at its face. A new element adds its row and column; a cell not yet decided reads *to do* until its rule is written, and none does now. Each code below names its rule in a few words, links to where the [rules](#rules) state it, which stay the authority, and lists the behaviour fixtures that play it (`scripts/fixtures/fixtures.ts`); each V rule links to [what the validator rejects](#what-the-validator-rejects) instead, so every cell is backed by a fixture or by the validator.

## Adding an element

Element types are described by data in `src/game/rules/`, but a new one still touches several files, because the chamber file reader, the validator, the walls' portal taking, the rays and the switching animation list elements by hand:

1. **Definition:** a file in `src/game/rules/elements/` that calls `defineElement` with its entry type. It gives the fixed properties (its name, its field in a chamber file, one entry or a list, the tiles it sits on, its draw layer, whether links reach it, its blocked flag's default or none, when it blocks walking, shots and shooting from its tile, when it stops a ray on its floor tile (`blocksRays`: never, or while blocked, as a light barrier that is on and a closed door or exit), when it takes portals, whether it reacts to the subject entering or leaving, whether entering it keeps the subject moving (`continuesMovement`), its events) and hooks for the rest (`interact`, `subjectInside`, `everyPass`, `linkUnblocked`, `linkBlocked`). Nothing reads when it takes portals (`takesPortals`): which tiles are walls and can take portals, and which of their sides (none facing a door or the exit, a laser or a light barrier running towards the wall, or a fizzler whose field spans its gap towards the wall, behind its connector), is written out in `src/game/rules/compiled_chamber.ts`, and whether a wall takes a portal now in `takesPortalsNow` (`step.ts`), so an element that sits on a wall, takes portals or keeps them off the walls beside it also touches both. An element that is a wall tile of its own, as the emitter and the receiver are, sits on `'wall'`: the step and the rays stop at it by reading the walls (`isWall`), and read the blocking properties only of elements on floor tiles, so its `blocksRays` is `'never'`; one that takes no portal on any face goes in `DEVICE_KINDS` in `compiled_chamber.ts`, which marks its tiles as walls but not as walls that take portals. An element that lines a gap a door, the exit or a fizzler fills, besides the walls, goes in `GAP_SIDE_KINDS` in `compiled_chamber.ts`, which decides which way such a gap is spanned (`gapSpan`): a door, the exit or a fizzler is drawn turned that way (see `docs/sprites.md`, turning), and the walls behind a fizzler's connectors, at both ends of its span, refuse portals (`canTakePortalOn`), so adding one can change where portals open, and with it the recorded solutions and fixtures. The rays read `blocksRays` in `src/game/rules/rays.ts`, which handles the subject, resting boxes and mirror boxes itself, so an element that stops a ray in another way, or turns one, touches it too. An element that destroys a box lying on it while blocked goes in `BOX_DESTROYING_KINDS` (`elements/box.ts`); the game draws such a box flying home in pieces, but on a ditch, where it falls, and, one a link reaches, breaking up where it lay (`src/game/destroyed_boxes.ts`).
2. **Registry:** its line in `ELEMENT_REGISTRY` (`element_registry.ts`), whose order is the drawing order within a layer, and also the order the elements' hooks run in (`step.ts`: each pass runs them type by type in registry order, so the receivers, after the buttons, are judged after them) and the order of the state's blocked flags, so moving a line can change the rules and the states' hashes, and with them the recorded solutions and fixtures. The element fields of `ChamberData`, the compiled chamber's lookups and the state's blocked flags follow from it. An element that keeps state beyond a blocked flag, as the receiver keeps its lit flag and the switch its used flag, needs its values in the state's layout (`state_layout.ts`), read in `state_reader.ts` and written in `state_draft.ts`, and in the solver's state key (`src/solver/state_key_codec.ts`), packed, as a switch's used flag is, or worked out again on unpacking, as a button's pressed flag and a receiver's lit flag are. One whose links act, as a button's, a switch's and a receiver's do, goes in `ActivatorKind` and `ACTIVATOR_KINDS` (`compiled_chamber.ts`), says when it is active in `isActivatorActive` (`state_reader.ts`), which the build asks for, and runs `applyLinks` (`state_draft.ts`) whenever that changes. One that walking alone can change, as walking into a ray darkens a receiver, breaks what the solver's macro search assumes, so it joins the receiver in the macro search's refusal (`macroSearchRefusal` in `src/solver/macro_search.ts`), which names the receiver by hand.
3. **Entry type:** a new one in `src/game/chamber_data.ts`, when none of the existing ones fits.
4. **Reader:** its field in `CHAMBER_FIELDS` and its list read in `chamber_parser.ts`.
5. **Validator:** its kind in `validate_chamber.ts`, placed on its tiles, and in the lists of what may share a tile, what may stand on the edge and what links can reach; a wall tile of its own among the wall kinds (`WALL_KINDS`), placed with the walls, and, when it faces a way, in the check that its face looks onto the floor (`checkDeviceFaces`); one with links in the link checks (`checkTriggers`), the check that nothing it links gives its blocked flag (`checkLinkedBlocked`) and the antline's first corner (`isLinkedFrom`); a kind whose name is more than one word, or starts with a vowel, also needs its wording in `describeKind`, or it reads "a lightBarrier" or "a emitter".
6. **Events:** each new one in `src/game/game_event.ts`, with its sound in `Level` (`src/game/level.ts`), or none.
7. **Drawing:** its entry in `ELEMENT_DRAWINGS` (`src/game/element_drawings.ts`), drawing its sprite for each state; each new sprite in the set (`src/game/sprite_set.ts` and `docs/sprites.md`), drawn in code as an SVG in `art/sprites/` and exported to its file in `src/assets/images/chamber/` (as `AGENTS.md` says) before it joins the set, since `npm run assets:check` requires every sprite's file, and in `RAISED_SPRITES` when it stands raised, so it gets the shared drop shadow; and, when links switch it, its kind in `TRANSITION_KINDS` and `TRANSITION_STYLES` (`src/game/element_transitions.ts`), with its drawing part way through the change, or it switches at once. The floor, floor marks and structure layers are drawn as the chamber's backdrop, drawn again only for a new tile size and when an antline lights up or goes dark, the floor layer's elements (the slide floors) under the antlines and the other two over them (`src/game/renderer.ts`), so an element on them must look the same in every state, or be left out of the backdrop and drawn every frame right over it instead, as the arrows a link turns are (`drawTurnableArrows` in `element_drawings.ts`, which also draws an arrow's turn part way, the arrow being in `TRANSITION_KINDS`). An antline lights while the button, switch or receiver it starts on is active, whatever the element it leads to, so a new linkable element needs nothing there; a wall tile of its own goes in `HIDING_WALL_KINDS` in the antline drawing (`src/game/antline_drawing.ts`), so no antline dots are drawn over it. Whether the surface detail's wear marks may land on a tile that holds it is its answer in `TAKES_WEAR` (`src/game/wear_marks.ts`), which the build asks for too (`docs/sprites.md`, surface detail); and whether each of its instances takes a look of its own, and which, is its answer in `LOOK_MAKERS` (`src/game/element_detail.ts`), null for none, which the build asks for as well, its drawing then passing each instance's look (`lookOf`) to the painter (`docs/sprites.md`, element detail). Each new browser-side module in `src/game/` (a drawing, an animation) goes in both ban lists of `eslint.config.js`, the rules' and the solver's, so neither can import it.
8. **Docs and fixtures:** its row in the table at the top, its field under [chamber format](#chamber-format), its rule under [rules](#rules) and [what the validator rejects](#what-the-validator-rejects), its row and column in the [interaction table](#interactions), every cell a rule, a case the validator forbids or *to do*, and any rule they need, each rule naming the fixtures that play it; behaviour fixtures in `scripts/fixtures/fixtures.ts`, and a case in `scripts/fixtures/format_examples.ts` for each new validator problem; and what it does and how it looks in `docs/game_summary.md`, and any guideline it needs in `docs/level_design.md`.
9. **Playground and gallery:** a place in Playground 1, the playground chamber for trying every element (`src/dev/playground/playground_1.json`, see `AGENTS.md`), switched by a button when it can be, so it can be tried by hand, and in the sprite gallery's specimen chamber (`src/dev/gallery_layouts.ts`), with everything on and everything off, so its sprites are checked in each state.
10. **Chamber sign:** its pictogram in `src/components/element_pictogram.vue` and its line in the sign's list (`src/components/chamber_sign.vue`), where it is shown dark in the chambers that hold it; it lays them out in rows of seven (the column count is fixed in the sign's styles), and its fourteen (the mirror box's among them, though it is a box) fill two rows, so the next starts a third row and makes the sign taller.

The recorded solutions change only when an existing chamber or its route changes. The build catches a missing drawing, sound, wear answer or look answer, and, for a kind added to `ActivatorKind`, a missing answer to whether it is active. `npm run chamber:check` catches a field missing from the reader's field list or used by two elements, an event with no owner or two, and an element that blocks walking, shots, shooting or rays only while blocked but has no blocked flag. Nothing catches a validator line, the list read in `chamber_parser.ts`, the chamber sign's line, or an entry in the other lists kept by hand, left out: `DEVICE_KINDS`, `GAP_SIDE_KINDS` and `ACTIVATOR_KINDS` (`compiled_chamber.ts`), `BOX_DESTROYING_KINDS` (`elements/box.ts`), `RAISED_SPRITES` (`sprite_set.ts`), `TRANSITION_KINDS` (`element_transitions.ts`), `HIDING_WALL_KINDS` (`antline_drawing.ts`), the lint ban lists, and the places in Playground 1 and the gallery's specimen chamber.
