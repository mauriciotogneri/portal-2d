## Run 2 in detail

The portal run: no new element, so no teach chamber, but one test, two twists and a combine (guideline 41), the combine sketched first, in the idea pool (guideline 51). Four chambers, not the five to seven guessed: run 2's consequences pair up, and one of them, the walls facing a door, can only be shown (guideline 42). Turning in place comes only in run 3, so every put-down and every shot a route needs works without the turn key (guideline 66). Walls alone can't force either portal to be the way in, or the portal just come out of to move, since the subject can shoot again any face it can walk into once that face is empty: the chambers that force them use the box (chamber 1.2's playtest).

## Why this shape

Each of the outline's consequences, and where it is taught:

| #   | Consequence                                                                                       | Chamber    | Note                                                                     |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | A shot moves only its own colour's portal, so either end can be moved, the one just come out of too | 2.3        | 2.1 only offers it, as walls alone can                                    |
| 2   | Either portal can be the way in (chamber 1.2's playtest)                                           | 2.4        | 1.4 already forces it in every route (decision 2)                         |
| 3   | The subject comes out of a portal facing away from its wall                                        | 2.1        | With where it comes out, and when it can't                                |
| 4   | The walls that never take a portal, such as those facing a door                                    | 2.2        | Shown where nothing is at stake; no run 2 chamber relies on it (guideline 75) |
| 5   | What an open door lets through                                                                     | 2.2 and 2.4 | Shots in 2.2; that a door shutting closes no portal in 2.4               |
| 6   | Planning portals around a box                                                                      | 2.3 and 2.4 | A box resting in front of a portal in 2.3; with the rest of run 2 in 2.4 |

The order: 2.1 brings back portals alone after 1.4's box; 2.2 is the run's easy chamber between 2.1 and 2.3 (guideline 46), two chambers before the combine, so what it shows is planted there, not rehearsed (guideline 47); 2.3 brings the box back into play just before the combine (guideline 48); 2.4 takes the training wheels off, with more walls that take portals than any chamber before it (guidelines 49 and 75).

Left out or merged:

- A fifth chamber, a twist from the idea pool that teaches the combine's key move on its own: left out, so the combine keeps an aha of its own instead of repeating one (guideline 47). Decision 1.
- A wall tile holding a portal refuses the other colour on its two neighbouring sides too (guideline 72): it looks like a bug, so no run 2 chamber lets it matter (guideline 9).
- Two portals facing one tile turn the subject in place: a one-off before the turn key arrives (guideline 6).
- Coming out of a portal facing a tile, to put the box down there without the turn key: a convenience in routes, never a key move, since a player who reads the controls legend just turns (guideline 38).
- A door shutting on a box in its doorway: with one box, that needs the button beside its door (guideline 59), and box loss is first shown in runs 4 and 5 (guideline 28).
- A box taking away the tile it rests on as a place to shoot from: it rarely bites, since the subject can shoot every face it can walk into from the tile in front of it; run 3's "a box bars the tile it rests on" covers it.
- Shots leaving through an open door or exit in the chamber's edge: met in run 1.

## Handbook lessons

Proposals only, for step 3.7.

| #   | Where                                  | Proposal                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| --- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1   | Portals, a new `Should`                | **Force which portal moves, and which is the way in.** With walls and ditches alone, any crossing can be made with either colour as the way in and either end moved, since the subject can shoot again any face it can walk into once that face is empty, so a lesson only offered costs one more shot to avoid, and goes unlearned (chamber 1.2's playtest). Forcing it takes the box: a box resting in front of the face just come out of forces that portal to move; a part whose only face holds the portal just come out of forces the way back through that colour, a trip worth making only for a box or a button |
| 2   | Portals, a new `Should`                | **Every face the subject can walk into, it can shoot.** Walking into a wall with no open portal pair on that side faces it, so the tile in front of a face always sees it, turn key or not: "the only tile that sees this face" is never the only one while the face's own tile can be reached, and a box taking away a tile to shoot from matters only for faces the subject comes out in front of rather than walks to                                    |
| 3   | Guideline 66, an addition              | Until turning in place is taught, shots need facing too: a tile a route shoots from, with floor ahead the way it shoots, needs a run-up behind it on that line or a portal behind it to come out of, since the move key moves the subject on; where the tile ahead can't be entered (a wall without a portal, an open ditch, a box), pressing that way only turns it                                                                       |
| 4   | Guideline 40, an addition              | A door shows one face per side: shots pass it only straight through its gap, so from each side the open doorway shows the first wall on that line and nothing else, and a shot along the wall from inside the doorway hits its frame, which never takes a portal. So the check is one line per side: is the button on the door's line, and does the first wall past the door on that line take portals                     |
| 5   | Guideline 78, an addition              | A part reached only by portal is safest with a single face: there the portal just come out of can't be lost from inside, since the other colour is refused on that face and can be shot nowhere else; with a second face, one shot of the wrong colour from inside strands the subject, unless the part also sees back to where it came from                                                                                |
| 6   | Patterns, "Five facts that shape them" | With one box and one button, the box has one job: links only open and a resting box opens nothing, so the box is only ever needed on that button, and every catch comes from where the box can go and how the subject gets around. "The box is needed elsewhere first" takes a second button, run 3's, or a second element on the same button, such as the exit                                                       |
| 7   | Guideline 9, an addition               | Walking into a portal whose other end faces an open ditch, or a box resting in front of it, is refused with the error sound, though that portal opened: add it to the refusals that look possible                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
